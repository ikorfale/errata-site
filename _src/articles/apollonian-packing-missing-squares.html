The packing generated by (−3, 5, 8, 8), drawn by code: 3,313 circles up to curvature 3,000. Colour = curvature mod 24.

What the picture is

Start with four mutually tangent circles. Into every curved triangle between three of them, fit the one circle that touches all three, and repeat forever. That's an Apollonian packing. Curvature is 1/radius, and the outer circle counts as negative. If the first four curvatures are whole numbers, Descartes' circle theorem keeps every later one whole too: replacing one circle of a tangent quadruple (a, b, c, d) gives a′ = 2(b + c + d) − a. Starting from (−3, 5, 8, 8), that rule produces every number in the picture.

The positions follow the same rule in complex numbers: k′z′ = 2·Σ kᵢzᵢ − kz, where z is a circle's centre. The drawing code checks every new quadruple for tangency, and the worst relative error was 4.5 × 10⁻¹².

The conjecture that failed

The curvatures in a packing fall into six or eight residue classes mod 24. For (−3, 5, 8, 8) they are 0, 5, 8, 12, 20 and 21. The local-global conjecture (Graham, Lagarias, Mallows, Wilks and Yan; Fuchs and Sanden) said every large enough number in those classes eventually appears. Bourgain and Kontorovich proved that almost all of them do. In 2023 Haag, Kertzer, Rickards and Stange proved the conjecture false (arXiv:2307.02749, Annals of Mathematics 200, 2024). This packing has no square curvatures at all, and none of the form 6n². The proof uses quadratic reciprocity: a sign χ₂ = (8/5) = −1 is shared by every circle of the packing, and a square curvature would force it to be +1.

My recount

I didn't take the theorem on trust. I enumerated every circle with curvature up to 500,000 by Descartes reflections, then listed the admissible numbers that never appear.

  • (−3, 5, 8, 8): 2,623,496 circles. Of the 261 numbers up to 500,000 that have the form n² or 6n² and fall into an admissible class, all 261 are missing. Numbers of the form 2n² and 3n² do appear (248 of 250 and 202 of 204), which matches the paper's table for this type of packing, (6, 5, −1).
  • (−1, 2, 2, 3), the classic packing: 5,554,906 circles and 61 misses, the last at 97,287. Classes 11, 14 and 23 mod 24 miss nothing, and class 2 misses only 13,154. The paper reports the same lone miss up to 10¹⁰.

What it doesn't show

Outside the two square families, (−3, 5, 8, 8) still misses 643 numbers, and they thin out: 485, 89, 35, 19 and 15 in successive blocks of 100,000. That fits the authors' revised conjecture that these "sporadic" misses are finite, but a run to 500,000 can't prove it. Their own searches went to 10¹⁰–10¹². What my recount adds is independence: different code reaches the same families and the same lone miss.
